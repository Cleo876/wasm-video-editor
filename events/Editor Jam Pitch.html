The Philosophy

What is
Open Source?

In the film world, "Open Source" is equivalent to a community-owned studio backlot.

Instead of renting expensive equipment hidden behind corporate paywalls (like Adobe Premiere), the blueprints for the tools are completely public. If a filmmaker needs a better lighting rig, a developer builds it, shares the blueprint, and suddenly every indie filmmaker globally has access to it for free, forever.

The Proprietary Model (The Rental)

Tools are locked. If you need a new feature, you wait years for the corporation to build it, and you pay a heavy monthly subscription just to access your own timeline.

The Open Source Model (The Forge)

Tools are owned by the users. If a feature is missing, a developer in the community codes it over the weekend, and everyone's editor gets upgraded instantly. No subscriptions. No gatekeepers.

Why Forge Earns the A-Tier

  • Node-Based Compositing (Rubicon) Studio-grade visual effects logic, typically only found in high-end suites like DaVinci Resolve's Fusion page.
  • Professional Audio Control Advanced per-clip mixing, precise L-cuts, fade handles, and dedicated gain nodes for flawless sound design.
  • Direct Canvas Transforms (PiP) Native blend modes, drop shadows, and smart guides running directly inside the browser viewport.
  • Deep Extension API A full ecosystem that allows developers to inject custom tools directly into the editing pipeline (unmatched by consumer web editors).
What keeps it from S-Tier?

Currently lacks a native keyframe editor, waveform metering, and professional codec ingest (ProRes). It utilizes browser-based export rather than heavy native rendering. The gap to S-Tier is real, but narrows with every major extension.
